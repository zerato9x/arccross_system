## PRIMAL CIVILIZATION

**Status:** Classified · unknown to everyone in-setting · never explicitly
revealed in play

An older civilization that understood Arc at a level later eras do not.

### The Transcendence

A wide **species event**. Most of the Primal Civilization undergoes
**The Transcendence**.

**The Primal Consciousness** (the persistent player identity in later eras)
**refuses** to transcend.

Instead, it **builds Arccross** as a way to **survive the onslaught** that
comes with / after that event.

Then the event arrives in full:

- Everything is engulfed in **Red Mist**.
- The Primal Civilization is **gone**.
- The Primal Consciousness **remains**.

**Pre-Arccross begins after this.**

### What this is not

Do **not** credit Primal Civilization with dated “firsts” that belong to later
eras:

| Not a Primal dated first | First appears in dated history |
| --- | --- |
| Mark system | Era I — North identifies / weaponizes / shares Mark |
| Arcborn as known category | Era I — North identifies Arcborn in the record |
| Handle technology | Era II — South develops first working Handle |

### Classified implications (designers only)

- Those who transcended may relate to why later worlds feel Earth-familiar
  (tech, bureaucracy, weapons) and why Arc telescopes point toward Earth —
  hold as implication, not UI exposition.
- No surviving public records of Primal society.
- **Primal Consciousness** ≠ a public historical nation-name. Do not print
  “Primal Civilization” in gameplay text.

**Player rule:** never name Primal Civilization or The Transcendence in UI.
Era V wrong-old ruins and Era IX Glitch fragments may hint without resolving.

## PRE-ARCCROSS

**Status:** Partially known  
**Begins:** After The Transcendence / Red Mist wipe of Primal Civilization;
Primal Consciousness remains on the world it built to endure.

A long, poorly remembered span before numbered eras.

**Confirmed:**

- Arccross exists as the survival construct / world left after Primal society
  is gone.
- Natural Arc energy is present.
- The Cores in each Arm light (or are found lit). Exact construction details
  of every Core remain sparse in the dated record.
- Civilization gathers around Core light — moths to flame.
- Origin of *later* humanity’s lineages on Arccross stays intentionally foggy
  in player-facing terms.

## Threat Taxonomy (locked)

These are **different** threats. Do not write Gaps/Cravens as “Automations
returned.”

| Threat | What it is | When it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Arc Automations** | Hive-minded machines clearing paths between Cores | Era I only as existential hive; **ended in Era I** |
| **Red Mist** | Planetary corruption / engulfing force (also tied to Transcendence end) | Recurring; expands through Gaps |
| **Gaps** | Breaches / wounds that let Mist pressure through | Escalates from Era V onward (North, then Man-Eater, then wider) |
| **Cravens** | Humans turned into mindless corrupted by Red Mist (“zombies”) | Appear with Mist/Gap crises (Zeta founded Era V end to fight them) |
| **Mist-monsters** | Animals turned into monsters by Red Mist | Same family of Mist corruption; not Automations |

## ERA I — Foundations / The Clearing

**Theme:** The world learns what a Core does when it acts.  
**Begins:** Gates of the Central Core open.  
**Ends:** Automation hive threat broken via shared Mark knowledge.

**Confirmed events:**

1. Central Core gates open to humanity’s dated age.
2. **Arc Automations** deploy — hive-minded machines whose purpose is to
   **clear a path to each Core by any means necessary**.
3. They demolish and enslave civilizations on the routes between Cores.
4. With primitive tech, **West, East, and South fall** under the Clearing
   (societies crushed; Cores remain lit moth-flames).
5. **North resists.**
6. North is first to identify **Arcborn** — treated as ordinary people under a
   curse, not yet a bureaucratic caste.
7. Under Automation threat, North **weaponizes the Mark** and **shares that
   knowledge** with settlements around the other Cores.
8. Shared Mark knowledge turns the tide; Automations are pushed back and
   **ended** as an existential threat. They do not return as the Mist/Gap/Craven
   problem of later eras.

**Residue for Era 9:** Central as the place that opened; cleared roads; fear
that Arc “help” is annihilation with a schedule.

## ERA II — Steel, Council, and the Collar

**Theme:** Shared victory becomes profitable blame.  
**Begins:** First Council seated; rotational stewardship of Central.  
**Ends:** Handle proven; Arcborn fear becomes common sense.

### Foundation

- Four Arms form a coalition with representatives.
- They found the **First Council**.
- Rule of **Central Core rotates** among the four — no permanent owner.
- First real government; first political utilization of Central.
- Official doctrine: come to Central to share knowledge and trade; **leave
  politics at the gate**.
- This myth of neutral Central is born here. Later eras violate it constantly.

### Undercurrent

- West and East suffer near-constant civil wars.
- Both Arms experiment on Arcborn and Marks as wartime leverage.
- **Merchant Guild** takes first roots in the South.
- Guild pushes hardest for Central’s government system (trade court).
- Guild enables the first real logistics web: steel West→East, East wares→West.
- Prolonged local conflict is good for margins. They are happy.

### Catalyst

- An elected **Western Council representative is assassinated**.
- Killer unknown (intentional blank).
- West and East enter a **massive war**; each blames the other.
- **North withdraws from politics** and isolates.
- South is surprised but capitalizes.

### Escalation

- Arcborn/Mark use goes extreme.
- Cities and monuments built across thousands of years are destroyed.
- South secretly develops the **Handler / Handle** system from battlefield
  data while East and West exhaust each other.

### End of the Great War

- Powerful Arcborn shatter most West and East organization and rule humans
  because they can.
- South emerges with the **first working Handle**, conquers rogue Arcborn, and
  “frees” West and East peoples.
- In practice: Arcborn tyranny replaced with Handle tyranny.
- **Most humans learn to fear Arcborn.** South writes the propaganda win.

**Era II sentence:** South didn’t end the age of monsters. They patented the
collar — and called it freedom.

## ERA III — First Bureaucracy / Converter Age

**Theme:** The collar becomes an org chart; Core heat becomes industry.  
**Begins:** Handle “liberation” order; Guild favor politics.  
**Ends:** Northern Core blast; North terraformed; mountains rise; Warden
founder entombed in crystal.

### Bureaucracy

- West and East mostly ruled by Southern puppets.
- Political game: who earns **Merchant Guild favor** wins the cake.
- **Handler Units** appear as offices, not secret labs.
- First full Arcborn exploitation system:
  - **West:** Arcborn as fodder for **Man-Eater Mountain**
  - **East:** Arcborn as slavery under Handle
  - **South:** licenses control via Handle + logistics

### Tech

- First **steam** age.
- First **Arc Converter**: Core energy → heat.
- Arc becomes industrial fuel; allotments become bureaucracy.

### North (still)

- Tribal, relative harmony.
- Region still **temperate** — not Crystal Valley, not arctic yet.

### Catalyst

- Discovery of Era I Automation motive: they were trying to **connect the
  Cores**.
- Civilization builds huge **pipelines** on old Automation schematics.
- Linked Cores amplify; Converter/steam age scales.
- South demands Northern cooperation to complete the grid.
- Northern tribals under **King Baric** refuse.
- South wages war under propaganda: Arcborn are dangerous; the North is
  danger. Real engine: finish the network.

### End

- Central Army marches on the Northern Core.
- Baric and elite Arcborn attempt to focus Northern Core power into a blast
  against Central. It backfires.
- A Central Army lieutenant Arcborn — later **founder of the Wardens** —
  interrupts the focus and **touches the Core** during detonation.
- Huge blast wipes Baric’s army and the Central Army.
- Blast **terraforms the North** into later arctic character.
- **Mountain ranges** rise that wall the North off from the rest.
- Founder is the **sole survivor**, **encased in a crystal tomb**.

**Era III sentence:** Someone tries to turn a Core into a gun — and the North
becomes the crater that proves Cores don’t forgive focus.

## ERA IV — Dual Rebuild / Electricity / The Passing Reopens

**Theme:** Funeral quiet, then greed opens the wall.  
**Begins:** Aftermath of the northern wipe.  
**Ends:** The Passing reopens for Arc-snow electricity.

### North track (isolated)

- Survivors from North and stranded Central remnants rebuild behind the
  mountains.
- Crystal-tomb **Warden founder** leads the rebuilding.
- Seedbed of later Northern / Warden culture. Off-grid; largely unknown south
  of the wall.

### South-of-wall track

- Guild recalculates hard: a Core can **explode**; Arcborn can **amplify**
  that power. Technology accelerates.
- Relatively peaceful — mostly because most fighting-age people died in the
  northern war. Manpower exhaustion, not virtue.
- Pockets of rebellion; mostly rebuilding.
- **Electricity** first appears at civilizational scale.

### End

- **The Passing** (way to the North) **reopens**.
- Motive: northern snow is ridden with **Arc-filled energy** that lets Core
  systems produce electricity at desired scale.
- Public language: reconnect / progress. Private language: harvest.

**Era IV sentence:** The quiet after a funeral — until someone realizes the
graveyard snow runs the lights.

## ERA V — Industrialization / Northern Frontier

**Theme:** Auction the North; plant the fuse.  
**Begins:** Passing open; industrial takeoff.  
**Ends:** Gaps confirmed in Man-Eater Mountain; Zeta Corps founded.

**Confirmed events:**

1. Arccross industrialization era. Era IV rebuild becomes a rush.
2. North becomes the **frontier** — competition of bidding contracts to
   explore.
3. Era III blast upheaval further affects the barrier that had protected the
   North from Red Mist; expansion/upheaval reveals thin clues of a previous
   civilization (Primal traces — never cleanly named).
4. Gold-rush mentality into northern yield (literal and Arc-wealth).
5. **Gaps** and **Red Mist** begin corrupting the northern vast region.
6. **Barican Coalition** (named after late King Baric) rules **Crystal
   Valley** — a deep settlement in the great arctic wasteland that exists as
   part of this frontier age. Its infrastructure is crucial to extracting
   Arc-filled snow.
7. Central tries to seize the land; labels Baricans terrorists; fails —
   weather + resistance.
8. Civilization absorbs Warden philosophy into bureaucracy: first **Warden
   Unit**.
9. Guild does what it does best: trades, agreements, contracts, secret deals
   with Baricans; fabricates raids; keeps Central posters and ledgers in
   different realities.
10. **End:** Gaps discovered in **Man-Eater Mountain**. Humanity realizes the
    crisis is not only northern. **Zeta Corps** formed to combat appearing
    **Cravens**.

**Era V sentence:** They industrialize the grave, auction the North, and call
Red Mist a mining hazard until the Gap grins inside the mountain they already
filled with bodies.

## ERA VI — Peak Arccross

**Theme:** Best gear into a leaking world.  
**Begins:** Inheritance of Era V systems pushed to maximum.  
**Ends:** Delta’s Barican mission “succeeds”; Passing sealed; North lost to
sight.

### Peak condition

- Tech pushed to max to deal with northern weather and Gaps appearing
  everywhere.
- Peak of Arccross infrastructure and general competence.
- Standard kit face: **Service Rifle** (Kar98k-class) and sidearm/equipment
  (M1911-class).
- Institutions at full burn: Warden Units, Zeta, Guild logistics, Core grid;
  Crystal Valley still the awkward northern keystone.
- Story pillars: **Warden 17**; **Delta** as era-ending Catalyst.

### End

- Delta’s mission: **stop deals with the Baricans; deny them legitimacy**
  (Crystal Valley is the Era V+ Barican Arc-snow keystone).
- He kind of does that — and in the process releases **huge Gaps** across
  northern lands; massive **Craven** hordes pour in.
- Humanity **seals The Passing again**.
- Last time the wider world sees the northern land as an open frontier (until
  much later archaeology / player-era exceptions).

**Era VI sentence:** Delta completes the paperwork — and the North answers with
Gaps; civilization’s last honest look north ends with a closed gate.

**Tech note for later eras:** Era VII stagnates on Era VI peak tools. Era VIII
Southern “high tech” (carbon weapons) exists but is not widely adopted.

## ERA VII — Cold War Stagnation / Civil War / Core Deaths

**Theme:** Same peak tools; politics and war shred the world.  
**Tech:** Stagnates on Era VI standard (Service Rifle world). No new general
age. North is behind a sealed Passing.

**Gist (confirmed framing):**

- Political Arms, Merchant Guild, and sealed-North aftermath coexist under
  rising tension.
- Academy / Mark / Xander program culture matures in this stagnant peak.
- Delta’s Era VII arc (after the northern seal): Master Trader killed; own
  Handler destroyed (survives); Free Mark trajectory; Arcborn rebellion.
- **Early / mid Era VII:** **Western Core** destroyed; **Man-Eater Mountain**
  sealed by Delta’s sacrifice redirecting Core detonation.
- Civil war: Arcborn rebellion escalates; infrastructure neglected; Arc
  maintenance declines.
- **End of Era VII:** **Eastern Core** destroyed in the Arcborn–Human war /
  Red Mist breach / Craven swarms.

## ERA VIII — The Last Empire / Long Decay

**Theme:** One empire starves slowly.  
**Political:** Merchant Guild / **Southern Core** as final surviving power.

**Confirmed framing:**

- No meaningful expansion; resources exhaust; population declines.
- Arc infrastructure decays; Red Mist expands.
- South develops a thin “high tech” edge: **carbon** weapons (in-game carbon
  rifle/pistol ≈ SCAR-H / Glock analogues).
- These exist; they are **not** widely adopted. Most material culture remains
  rotting Era VI peak.
- The empire does not explode. It slowly starves.
- Civilization shrinks toward **Central** under Mist, extremes, and neglect.

**Era VIII sentence:** The last empire invents better toys for a funeral and
forgets to feed the grid.

## ERA IX — The Glitch (Playable Present)

**Theme:** The corpse of history twitches in the repair queue.  
**Begins:** Start of Era 9 = end of Era 8’s long decay.  
**Player era.**

### Opening condition

- Long decay completed its work. Outer Arms emptied; people concentrated into
  Central.
- Central houses residual population and the last functioning civil Core seat.
- Capacity maxed. Through bureaucratic triage, an ordinary resident (**Steve** /
  Catalyst Agent) is **evicted**.
- Survive → repair infrastructure → settlements return → reconnect logistics →
  restore Arc Core network → recover lost history.

### The Glitch

- Fragments of past eras begin to **appear** in the present.
- Not UI flashbacks. Wrong continuity in places, objects, documents,
  institutions, and anomalies:
  - Era VI peak kit beside Era VIII rare carbon
  - Guild paper beside Council slogans
  - Converter bones beside Automation path-logic
  - Sealed-North horror residue beside Central overcrowding theater
  - Primal wrong-old traces without names
- Arms in Era 9 are not live rival capitals. They are **fragment dialects** —
  wreckage of different centuries coexisting because decay and the Glitch will
  not let one era own the map.

### Meta

- Player is not Steve. Player is the persistent observer.
- Steve dies. Infrastructure remains. Knowledge accumulates.
- Long-term realization: civilization was never saved by heroes. It was
  rebuilt by ordinary workers maintaining extraordinary infrastructure.
